## Not verified here

- absolute range (needs a reflector at a taped distance); the presets agree with each other
- velocity scale (needs a moving target); a still reflector reads 0 m/s
- angles: the antenna configuration follows TI's SWRU639 figures; needs a corner-reflector calibration
- breathing and heart rate against a reference (chest belt, pulse oximeter)

## Validation of the vital-signs preset against a reference (2026-10-09)

One person seated still 1 m in front of the radar, 60 s, `Vital signs - 1 person, 100 fps`
(take and `validation.json` in `~/radar_measurements/cascade/validation_2026-10-09`). The radar
was evaluated blind, before the reference values were known.

| | radar (chest at 1.11 m) | reference |
|---|---|---|
| breaths in 60 s | 9 (peaks of the 0.08-0.7 Hz band) | 9 (counted by the subject) |
| heart rate | 71-75 bpm in 10 s windows, 72.8 bpm over the minute; `tools.heart_rate` 72 bpm | 71-75 bpm (Apple Watch) |

The breathing was irregular (3-9 s between breaths): the spectral breathing rate came out at
6.8 /min, counting the breaths gives the right 9. DARTscope's Fastcheck found a regular rhythm
in only 2 of 21 windows and `tools.vital_range_scan` picked a reflection at 2.15 m instead of the
chest - weaknesses of those two algorithms, not of the data. Both were fixed on 2026-10-10:
Fastcheck now counts the breaths (9 /min, 9 breaths counted, clear in 20 of 21 windows, heart
72 bpm) and the scan weights bins by how much they move (chest at 1.11 m, 9 breaths counted,
heart 73 bpm); on 45 older vital-signs recordings the scan found a seated person within 0.3 m
of the known distance in 19 of 21 (9 before). One person, one minute: more
subjects, longer takes and a time-resolved reference (chest belt, ECG) are still to come.
